More than 300 million people around the world live with a rare disease. These conditions are challenging to study, because each affects a small number of geographically dispersed patients. As a result, treatment options for many rare diseases remain out of reach.

Our rare disease tissue registry—a repository of genomic data, tissue, and blood samples—provides essential resources to help researchers and clinicians learn more about these conditions. By bringing together specimens and clinical information from multiple rare disease populations, the registry supports both disease-specific investigations and research into biological mechanisms shared across conditions.

Through this registry, we aim to build lasting research infrastructure for the entire rare-disease community—transforming individual contributions into a carefully curated resource that may advance scientific understanding and create a stronger foundation for future diagnostics and therapies.

Information for Patients

Our rare disease registry gives patients a chance to make a lasting contribution to medical research. Every sample we collect strengthens our understanding of rare diseases and brings treatment options closer.

Patient privacy and responsible stewardship are central to our efforts. We assign unique study codes to biospecimens and research information, access is limited to authorized personnel, and external research use is governed by appropriate scientific review, participant consent, ethics oversight, and data or material transfer agreements. Through these safeguards, we protect your privacy while enabling you to support meaningful research for years to come.

The registry is observational and noninterventional. It does not assign treatment, administer an experimental therapy, or alter a participant’s medical care. Depending on the participant and the availability of suitable materials, the registry may include leftover tissue from clinically indicated procedures, previously collected specimens, or a small blood sample obtained in accordance with the approved study protocol. Relevant clinical information may also be collected so that specimens can be studied in the proper context.

For more information about participating in our rare disease registry, please contact us.

Information for Researchers and Clinicians

Our rare disease registry provides an ethically governed resource to help academic, biotechnology, pharmaceutical, and other qualified researchers learn more about rare diseases and explore treatment options. It supports biomarker discovery, genotype–phenotype research, disease characterization, assay development, and future diagnostic and therapeutic research.

The registry may also help researchers identify patterns in disease progression, understand why individuals with the same condition experience different outcomes, and determine which areas warrant more focused investigation.

To learn more about how you can access this resource, please contact us.
